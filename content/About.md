# About the Collection

## Why I Created This Collection

Mental health in the aviation industry is something I have experienced firsthand as a flight attendant. I have been working as a flight attendant for two years, and during that time I have experienced many of the working conditions discussed throughout this collection, including fatigue, irregular sleep schedules, workplace stress, difficult work-life balance, and the effects that an airline and base can have on quality of life.

Because of that experience, I wanted to create a collection that looks at these issues beyond the surface level. Mental health in aviation is connected to the way the job is structured, the environment employees work in, and the demands placed on them. These factors can affect how people feel both while they are working and outside of work.

## What You'll Find Here

This collection brings together credible research and resources about mental health and well-being in aviation, with a primary focus on flight attendants.

The collection is organized into four main areas:

[**Occupational Stress & Work Environment**](stress) 
Explores workplace stress, working conditions, workplace relationships, and work-life conflict.

[**Burnout, Fatigue & Sleep**](burnout) 
Focuses on burnout, fatigue, irregular schedules, insomnia, and the effects of disrupted sleep.

[**Psychological Health & Trauma**](trauma) 
Examines psychological distress, mental health, PTSD, trauma, and the effects of critical incidents.

[**Support & Well-Being**](support) 
Highlights mental-health resources, peer support, employee assistance programs, critical-incident response, and other forms of support available within aviation.

Each source includes an annotation explaining what it covers, why it is relevant, who may benefit from it, and any limitations or considerations to keep in mind.

## Who This Is For

This collection is intended for flight attendants, aviation professionals, aviation students, researchers, and anyone interested in understanding the mental-health challenges connected to working in aviation.

My goal is not to suggest that every flight attendant will have the same experience. Instead, this collection provides research and resources that can help readers better understand the occupational factors that may affect mental health and well-being.

## How to Use This Collection

You can browse the collection by category or explore individual sources based on the topics that interest you. Tags help connect related topics across categories, making it easier to see how issues such as stress, fatigue, burnout, trauma, and support can overlap.

I hope this collection can serve as both an informative resource and a starting point for anyone who wants to learn more about the relationship between aviation work and mental health.
