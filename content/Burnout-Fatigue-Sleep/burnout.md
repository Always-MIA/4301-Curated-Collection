# Burnout, Fatigue & Sleep

Fatigue and sleep disruption are some of the most recognizable challenges of airline work. Early report times, late-night arrivals, changing time zones, reserve schedules, long duty periods, and constantly changing schedules can make consistent sleep difficult. Over time, these conditions can contribute to burnout and affect both work performance and quality of life.

---

## Factors Related to Burnout and Its Effects on Mental and Physical Symptoms and Daytime Dysfunction Among Female Flight Attendants

**Source:** Hu, C. J., Chen, Y. J., & Hong, R. M. (2023). _Factors Related to Burnout and Its Effects on Mental and Physical Symptoms and Daytime Dysfunction Among Female Flight Attendants._
**Type:** Peer-Reviewed Research

**What it covers:** This study examined burnout among 412 female flight attendants working for an international airline. Researchers found that 19.2% of participants reported a moderate level of burnout and examined the effects of burnout on mental-health symptoms, physical symptoms, and daytime functioning.

**Why it is valuable:** This source demonstrates that burnout is not simply feeling tired after a difficult trip. It can affect mental health, physical symptoms, and daytime functioning. This makes it especially relevant when considering how repeated work demands can accumulate over time.

**Who would benefit:** Flight attendants, aviation students, airline employers, and occupational-health researchers would benefit from this source.

**Limitations:** The study only included female flight attendants from one international airline and relied on self-reported questionnaire data, which limits how broadly the results can be applied.

## Insomnia, Work-Related Burnout, and Eating Habits Affecting the Work Ability of Flight Attendants

**Source:** Hu, C. J., Hong, R. M., Yeh, G. L., & Hsieh, I. C. (2019). _Insomnia, Work-Related Burnout, and Eating Habits Affecting the Work Ability of Flight Attendants._
**Type:** Peer-Reviewed Research

**What it covers:** This study examined 472 flight attendants and investigated how insomnia, work-related burnout, eating habits, and other factors were associated with work ability. The researchers found that insomnia and work-related burnout were negatively associated with work ability, while healthier eating habits were positively associated with it.

**Why it is valuable:** This source connects two issues that are easy to separate—sleep and burnout—and shows how both can affect an employee's ability to perform their job. This connection is particularly relevant to flight attendants because irregular schedules can make recovery between trips difficult.

**Who would benefit:** Flight attendants, aviation students, airline managers, occupational-health researchers, and anyone interested in fatigue management would benefit from this study.

**Limitations:** The study was cross-sectional, so it identifies relationships rather than proving that insomnia or burnout directly causes lower work ability.

[Learn More](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31227032/)

## Fatigue and Sleep in Airline Cabin Crew: A Scoping Review

**Source:** Wen, C. C. Y., Cherian, D., Schenker, M. T., & Jordan, A. S. (2023). _Fatigue and Sleep in Airline Cabin Crew: A Scoping Review._
**Type:** Peer-Reviewed Review

**What it covers:** This scoping review examined 27 studies about fatigue, sleepiness, sleep quality, and mental health among airline cabin crew. The review found high levels of fatigue and sleepiness, poor sleep quality, and concerns surrounding sleep disorders. It also identified evidence connecting cabin-crew work with mental-health concerns such as depression and anxiety.

**Why it is valuable:** Instead of focusing on one airline or one study, this source brings together a broad body of existing research. It helps identify patterns across the literature and shows how scheduling, rosters, individual differences, and coping strategies can influence outcomes.

**Who would benefit:** Flight attendants, researchers, aviation students, airline management, and anyone interested in the relationship between fatigue, sleep, and mental health would find this especially useful.

**Limitations:** The review notes that much of the existing research focuses on international or long-haul flights and that many studies include predominantly female participants.

[Learn More](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36768014/)

## Flight Attendant Fatigue: A Quantitative Review of Flight Attendant Comments

**Source:** Federal Aviation Administration. (2011). _Flight Attendant Fatigue: A Quantitative Review of Flight Attendant Comments._
**Type:** Government Report

**What it covers:** This FAA report analyzed 1,800 comments from a federally mandated survey of flight attendants. The comments were organized into categories including scheduling, health, airline policies, job performance and satisfaction, workload, meals, and break facilities. The report provides insight into concerns directly raised by flight attendants.

**Why it is valuable:** This source is valuable because it allows flight attendants to speak through their own survey comments. Rather than relying only on researchers' interpretations, the report identifies workplace issues employees themselves described as important.

**Who would benefit:** Flight attendants, airline management, aviation researchers, students, and anyone studying fatigue or working conditions in the airline industry would benefit.

**Limitations:** The report was published in 2011, so some airline policies and working conditions may have changed. It also focuses specifically on fatigue rather than mental health as a whole.

[Learn More](https://www.faa.gov/data_research/research/med_humanfacs/oamtechreports/2010s/2011/201116)
