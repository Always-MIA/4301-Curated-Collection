# Support & Well-Being

Understanding aviation-related mental-health challenges is only part of the conversation. Flight attendants and other aviation professionals also need access to meaningful support when they are struggling. This section focuses on peer support, employee assistance programs, critical incident response, and industry approaches to improving well-being.

---

## IATA — Wellbeing

**Source:** International Air Transport Association (IATA). _Wellbeing: Flight Crew, Cabin Crew, ATCOs, Engineers & All Roles._
**Type:** Aviation Organization Resource

**What it covers:** IATA's wellbeing resource explains why the mental health and well-being of aviation employees are relevant to aviation safety. It discusses professional and personal stressors and connects employee well-being with factors such as decision-making, situational awareness, and organizational resilience. The resource also provides information about peer support and psychosocial risk management.

**Why it is valuable:** This source expands the conversation beyond individual coping and shows that employee well-being can also be an organizational and safety issue. It supports the idea that airlines and aviation organizations have a role in creating healthier working environments.

**Who would benefit:** Flight attendants, airline managers, aviation students, safety professionals, and researchers would benefit from this industry-level perspective.

**Limitations:** IATA represents the international aviation industry, so its guidance is broad and may not reflect the specific policies or resources available at an individual airline.

[Learn More](https://ic.iata.org/capture/safety-issue/87)

## APFA Employee Assistance Program & Critical Incident Response Program

**Source:** Association of Professional Flight Attendants (APFA). _Employee Assistance Program / Critical Incident Response Program._
**Type:** Aviation Organization / Professional Resource

**What it covers:** APFA's Employee Assistance Program provides support specifically for American Airlines flight attendants. Its Critical Incident Response Program provides confidential crisis intervention following critical incidents and workplace trauma. The program uses trained flight-attendant peer representatives and provides support for employees dealing with difficult experiences.

**Why it is valuable:** This resource shows what mental-health support can look like within an actual flight-attendant organization. It moves the collection from identifying workplace problems to examining practical systems designed to help employees after difficult or traumatic experiences.

**Who would benefit:** Flight attendants, airline employees, aviation students, union representatives, and organizations developing employee-support programs would find this resource useful.

**Limitations:** The program is specific to APFA and American Airlines flight attendants. Its structure may differ from employee-assistance or critical-incident programs at other airlines.

[Learn More](https://apfa.dev/resources/eap/)

## Pilot Peer Support Programme Guide

**Source:** International Air Transport Association (IATA) / European Pilot Peer Support Initiative (EPPSI). (2020). _Pilot Peer Support Programme Guide._

**Type:** Aviation Organization Guide

**Tags:** `#IATA` `#PeerSupport` `#MentalHealth` `#Aircrew` `#AviationOrganization`

**What it covers:** This guide explains how formal peer-support programs can provide pilots with confidential assistance from trained colleagues when they experience mental-health or life-stress concerns. It discusses trained peer representatives, mental-health professionals, confidentiality, organizational trust, and creating an environment where aviation employees feel comfortable seeking help.

**Why it is valuable:** Although the guide focuses on pilots rather than flight attendants, its peer-support framework is relevant to the broader aviation industry. It provides an example of how aviation organizations can create structured systems that encourage employees to seek help before problems become more serious.

**Who would benefit:** Aviation professionals, airline managers, flight attendants, pilots, safety professionals, and students interested in workplace mental-health programs would benefit from this guide.

**Limitations:** The program is designed around pilots, so not every recommendation can be directly transferred to cabin crew. It is also a framework rather than evidence that a specific program produces better mental-health outcomes.
[Learn More](https://ic.iata.org/sites/default/files/iata_sih_document_attachment/EPPSI-Guide-2nd-Edition-October-2020.pdf)

## Fatigue in Aviation

**Source:** Federal Aviation Administration (FAA). _Fatigue in Aviation._ 16:22.
**Type:** Government Video

**What it covers:** This FAA video explains fatigue as an aviation safety and human-performance issue. It is part of the FAA's official Aircrew Health and Safety video collection and discusses the effects of fatigue on aviation personnel. The FAA lists _Fatigue in Aviation_ as an award-winning 16-minute video alongside other aviation physiology and health resources.

**Why it is valuable:** This video adds a different format to the collection and gives visitors an accessible way to learn about fatigue without reading an academic paper. It also reinforces the connection between employee health, alertness, performance, and aviation safety.

**Who would benefit:** Flight attendants, pilots, aviation students, aviation professionals, and anyone who wants an accessible introduction to fatigue in aviation would benefit from this resource.

**Limitations:** The video addresses aviation fatigue broadly rather than focusing specifically on flight attendants or mental health. It is primarily educational rather than a research study measuring outcomes in a specific population.

[Learn More](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/training/airman_education/physiologyvideos)
